Layout template

Sidebar

Rohan Jadhav is measuring a bore against 48.00 +0.05 / −0.00. The tolerance, the gauge and the sampling plan are not something he reads once and remembers — he checks them against every reading, and a screen that makes him leave the entry form to see them will be a screen he stops using correctly. This layout keeps a narrower secondary region continuously associated with a dominant content region, for exactly that case: material that earns lateral persistence. What the sidebar contains is deliberately not prescribed — it is not defined as navigation.

§01

Purpose

Unconditional

Keep a secondary region continuously associated with a dominant content region, where the secondary material benefits from lateral persistence. The test is continuity of reference: does the user consult the secondary region while working in the main one, repeatedly, in a way that makes moving away from it a cost? If the answer is no, the material is supplementary and belongs in the flow of single column.

Appropriate

  • Reference consulted per action — drawing tolerances beside measurement entry, a rate card beside a quotation.
  • Refinement controls beside the collection they narrow, where the current criteria must stay visible.
  • Context about the record being edited: revision history, the approval chain, related documents.
  • Any content whose value is that it is still there after the next interaction.

Not this layout

  • A collection whose selection drives the other region. That is master–detail: the lateral region is not reference, it is the thing being chosen from.
  • Two independent lateral regions — controls on one side, context on the other. That is a holy-grail frame and has its own ordering rules.
  • Material read once. Notes, related records, history that informs but is not consulted mid-task: single column, subordinate in the flow.
  • Space that happens to be free. A region invented to occupy 1600px is not a hierarchy.

Applicable interaction patterns. Primary: none — the frame hosts patterns rather than implying one. Supported: collection refinement, search results, explicit save. Where refinement is the pattern, the sidebar holds the controls and the main region the collection — but the criteria model, the counts and the clearing semantics are the pattern's, and this frame adds only that the two stay side by side.

§03

Properties

Collapsed is a user state, never a default the product ships in.

Presence of the page header and the dividers is composition, not configuration. The sidebar's content is not a property of this frame at all.

States

These four are the whole state model. Anything else on the screen — a pending refinement, a dirty draft in the main region — belongs to the pattern in play, and this frame neither holds nor coordinates it.

§08

Accessibility

Unconditional
  • Responsive relocation must preserve a meaningful reading and focus order. Whichever side the sidebar takes, document order and visual order agree, and the relocated single-column order is one a person can work in: reference before the form it is read against, context after the content it describes. Relocation is a change of place, never a scramble.
  • The Drawer route inherits Drawer's focus management — trap while open, restoration to the trigger on close, dismissal routes. This frame adds nothing to it and must not reimplement any of it.
  • A control that exposes the sidebar communicates state and relationship: aria-expanded reflecting whether region 2 is showing, and aria-controls naming it while it is in the document — when the region is genuinely removed (collapsed, or a closed Drawer), the attribute is omitted rather than left pointing at nothing, because a dangling IDREF is worse than no relationship. Its label says what appears — "Show tolerances" — not "Toggle sidebar".
  • Landmarks are assigned by role, not by column. Region 3 is main. Region 2 takes aside, or search/form when that is genuinely what it is, and carries an accessible name in every state, because a second unnamed region is indistinguishable from the first.
  • Heading levels run down the page, not across the split. The page header owns the h1; both regions start their own sections at h2. Lateral position is not hierarchy.
  • Both regions scroll to their own content without trapping. If a region scrolls independently (§13), keyboard focus moving into it must bring the focused control into view — a sidebar that scrolls only by pointer is unusable.
§09

Motion

Conditional — included: the frame's structure changes under §06
  • Collapse and restore are the only motion this frame owns. Region 2 leaves and returns along its own axis — width, not opacity — so the main region visibly takes and gives back the space. A fade reads as content vanishing; a slide reads as a region moving.
  • Use the system's short duration and standard easing. Motion here is orientation, not expression: long enough to see where the region went, short enough that the next reading is not waiting on it.
  • The Drawer route uses Drawer's own enter and exit. Do not animate the frame underneath it as well.
  • Responsive relocation is not animated. A layout re-laying out at a breakpoint is not an event the user caused, and animating it turns a resize into a performance.
  • The region leaves the document when the motion ends, not when it starts. Region 2 stays mounted for the duration of the collapse — marked aria-hidden while it is on its way out — and is removed once the width reaches zero, so §06's contract and the animation do not contradict each other.
  • Reduced motion: the region appears and disappears without transition, and leaves the document in the same frame. The state change is still announced and still legible — nothing about the contract depends on the animation.
§11

Responsive

Unconditional
  • Expanded. Sidebar and main region coexist laterally. Surplus width goes to the main region; a fixed or bounded sidebar does not grow with the viewport.
  • Constrained. The sidebar normally moves before or after the main region in one column — reference material before, contextual material after — or becomes available through a Drawer where temporary access preserves the intended hierarchy better than a long scroll past it. Pick one per screen and state it; do not offer both.
  • The floor is the rule. The primary content must retain usable width rather than being compressed indefinitely. When the main region reaches its floor, the sidebar relocates — the split does not keep narrowing until both regions are unusable.
  • Relocation is not a different layout. Same regions, same contents, same names, same order logic. A constrained-width sidebar that loses half its content was two designs.
  • Collapsible is a user choice, not a breakpoint. Do not collapse the sidebar automatically at some width and call it responsive: that is relocation, and it must follow the rules above rather than silently hiding reference material the user is relying on.
Do — relocate at the floor
2 · sidebar, before main
3 · main, full usable width
On a tablet at the goods-inward gate the tolerances come first, then the entry form at full width. Nothing is lost and nothing is cramped.
Don't — squeeze both to keep the split
2 · sidebar
3 · main
Lateral persistence is not worth a 300px entry form. The measurement fields wrap, the tolerance text goes to three words a line, and the operator gives up on both.
§12

Anti-patterns

Unconditional
  • Using it merely to fill unused horizontal space. The sidebar is justified by continuous reference, not by available pixels.
  • Reaching for it when supplementary material needs no persistent spatial separation. Use single column: subordinate placement in the flow says "reference", and says it honestly.
  • Using it when the lateral region is a collection whose selection drives the other region. That is master–detail, and its contract — selection identity, correspondence, stale content, empty state — is specified nowhere in this frame.
  • Using it when two independent lateral regions are structurally required. That is a holy-grail frame; forcing both into one sidebar makes the region a bucket.
  • Compressing the main region to preserve the split. Named again here because it is the failure this layout actually ships with.
  • A sidebar that scrolls the whole page away. Region 2 that is only reachable by scrolling past region 3 is not persistent; it is a footer.
  • Collapsed by default. A frame whose defining region starts hidden is a single column with an extra control.
  • Assuming the sidebar is navigation and giving it navigation chrome — a nav landmark, current-page treatment — when it holds reference content. The role must match what is in it.
§13

Implementation notes

Unconditional
  • Two tracks, one declaration. Build the split with Grid/GridItem or a nested Stack/Box structure; Container, Surface and Divider establish the boundaries. The sidebar track is a fixed or bounded length, the main track is minmax(<floor>, 1fr). That minmax floor is the §11 contract expressed in one value — without it, the frame silently squeezes instead of relocating.
  • Both tracks need min-width: 0. A table or a long part number in either region will otherwise push the track past its declared width and break the split from inside.
  • Document order follows sidebarSide. Order the regions in the markup; do not place them visually with order or grid line numbers against their DOM sequence, or reading order and visual order part company at exactly the width where it matters.
  • Independent scroll is opt-in and explicit. Either the page scrolls as one, or each region owns its own scroll container with its own height constraint. Two scrollbars by accident — a region that scrolls inside a page that also scrolls — is the most common defect in this frame.
  • A collapsible sidebar's track sizes to its content (auto), so the region's own width transition moves the boundary. A fixed track cannot be animated reliably across engines, and animating grid-template-columns instead makes the frame's structure the animated thing — put the motion on the region, not on the grid.
  • A sticky sidebar sticks within its own track, not to the viewport, and never overlaps the main region's content or its own scroll.
  • The Drawer route is the same content mounted elsewhere, not a second implementation of the sidebar. Keep one component and give it a home in both states, or the two copies will drift within a release.
  • The frame holds only §06's state. Collapsed and drawer-open live here; everything the regions contain holds its own state, and the layout passes nothing between them.

Omitted, with reasons — §04 Variants: one structural form; side, width and persistence are properties (§03), and the constrained-width forms are §11, not variants. · §05 Sizes: no frame-level size classes; the sidebar track is a §03 property and everything else comes from the spacing and density tokens. · §07 Keyboard: the frame introduces no keyboard relationship between regions — focus runs in document order, the toggle is an ordinary Button, and the Drawer route's focus trap and restoration are Drawer's contract, cited in §08 rather than restated. · §10 Content rules: the frame constrains no content beyond each region's purpose in §02; what belongs in the sidebar is a product decision this layout deliberately leaves open. · §14 Developer notes: nothing technical remains once §13 is written — no slot API, no routing or persistence responsibility beyond §06's two flags, no per-region data boundary.
