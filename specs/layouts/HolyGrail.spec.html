Layout template

Holy grail

Anjali Deshmukh releases the week's press work at 09:40 on a Tuesday. Two different things decide what she releases and neither is the queue itself: the criteria that narrow it, and the capacity and material position that say whether what is left can actually be run. Those are not two halves of one panel — one is input to the queue, the other is a constraint on acting on it — and moving either off screen turns the decision into two screens and a memory. This layout is the frame for exactly that: a dominant central region flanked by two lateral regions with genuinely independent semantic purposes, optionally between a top and a bottom region.

§01

Purpose

Unconditional

Support a central primary workspace that simultaneously requires two different classes of lateral information or control. The established convention is precisely this — a central flexible region between two side regions, historically with a top and a bottom region around them — and the convention is worth keeping only where the two sides do different work. The test is independent semantic purpose: name what each lateral region is for in one clause without using the other's words. Criteria that narrow the queue, and constraints that gate acting on it, pass. Filters on the left and more filters on the right do not.

Appropriate

  • Refinement controls on one side, the consequences of the refined set on the other — load, cost, exposure — with the collection in the centre.
  • A workspace where one side supplies what goes in and the other reports what the current state implies.
  • Two lateral regions each consulted repeatedly during the same task, where losing either costs a screen change.

Not this layout

  • One persistent secondary region. Use sidebar; a second column invented for symmetry is not a purpose.
  • Three content groups. Having three things to show is not having two lateral roles.
  • Global navigation + local navigation + content. Navigation-system architecture is outside this catalog and does not, by itself, call for this frame.
  • A lateral collection whose selection replaces the centre. That is master–detail wearing three columns.

Applicable interaction patterns. Primary: none — the frame hosts patterns rather than implying one. Supported: collection refinement, search results. Where refinement is the pattern, the leading region holds the criteria and the centre the collection; the criteria model, the counts and the clearing semantics stay the pattern's, and this frame adds only that a second, differently-purposed region stays beside them.

§02

Anatomy

Unconditional
#RegionRequirementWhat it owns
1Outer containerRequiredThe frame's outer measure and gutter, and the three-track body structure. One per screen; regions 2–6 are its children and nothing else is.
2Top regionOptionalSpans the body. States the subject of the whole screen and owns the h1. If it is present it spans all three body regions — a top region over only part of the body is region 4's own header, not this region.
3Leading regionRequiredThe first lateral region and its declared purpose, its width (§03), its persistence, and its own internal order. Its function is not prescribed: controls, criteria, reference, an index. It must be nameable in one clause.
4Main regionRequiredThe dominant, flexible centre. Owns the usable-width floor (§13): it is the track that flexes, and it is never compressed so that both lateral regions can stay.
5Trailing regionRequiredThe second lateral region, with a purpose independent of region 3's. Duplicating region 3's role here does not justify the template.
6Bottom regionOptionalSpans the body. Where the screen's committing action or its running summary lives. Reserves its height whether or not it has anything to say, so the centre does not reflow when it fills.
7DividersOptionalClarify the two body boundaries where surface and gap do not. Never load-bearing: a frame legible only because of two rules has a spacing problem.

The centre stays dominant. Three regions in a row is the shape that most easily loses its subject: if the two lateral tracks together are wider than the centre, the screen no longer has a primary workspace and the reader cannot tell which region they are meant to be working in.

Properties

Conditional — included: four choices change the frame's contract

Proportional (minmax(240px, 0.24fr)) only where the region's content genuinely scales with the viewport. Either way it carries a minimum, below which §11 displaces rather than squeezes.
trailingWidthfixed/bounded track · proportionalbounded, 320pxSet independently of the leading region: the two sides hold different material and there is no reason for them to be the same width. Symmetry here is a coincidence, not a rule.
mainMinWidthusable-width floor, px560pxThe measure below which the centre stops being a workspace. It is a declared number, not an outcome: it is what makes the §11 displacement mandatory rather than a judgement call, and it is expressed literally as the centre track's minmax floor (§13).
lateralPersistenceper region: persistent · collapsibleboth persistentPersistent is the point of the frame. Collapsible is legitimate where the centre occasionally needs the full measure — a wide table, a drawing — and is then set per region, because the two sides are not equally dispensable. The control that hides a region must restore it and must say which state it is in (§08). Collapsed is a user state, never a shipped default. A close control inside a lateral region is admitted only where that region is collapsible, and only in addition to the toggle that restores it — a region set persistent carries no dismiss affordance at all, because the frame promises it is there. In the Drawer route, add none: Drawer owns its own close.

Presence of the top region, the bottom region and the dividers is composition, not configuration. What either lateral region contains is not a property of this frame — only that the two purposes differ (§01).

§06

States

Conditional — included: the frame holds states no component in it holds
StateWhenContract
three-regionWide width, both lateral regions persistent or expandedAll three body regions co-visible, centre dominant. The frame's normal state and the one it exists for.
collapsedA region set collapsible, user hid itThat region leaves the visual and accessibility trees — not merely hidden. Its toggle stays, reports aria-expanded="false", and restores the region to the scroll and internal state it had. Held per region: one side collapsed is not both.
displacedConstrained width (§11), stacking routeA lateral region sits before or after the centre in one column — same region, same contents, same accessible name. Nothing about it is a different component.

The states are per lateral region, and the pair is not symmetric: the frame must be able to be in trailing displaced, leading present — that is the whole intermediate width — so an implementation that stores one flag for "lateral regions hidden" cannot express §11. Everything else on the screen belongs to the pattern in play; this frame neither holds nor coordinates it.

§08

Accessibility

Unconditional
  • DOM order is meaningful document order, not the visual left-to-right sweep. Order the regions as they are meant to be read: material used before acting precedes the centre, material about the centre's current state follows it. Where the two disagree, the reading order wins and the visual placement is achieved with tracks, never by re-ordering the markup.
  • Every responsive form keeps a working order. The stacked form (§11) must read as a task, not as a column emptied top to bottom: criteria, then the work, then what the work implies. An order that is right at desk width and scrambled on a tablet is an unmet contract.
  • Landmarks by role, names always. Region 4 is main. Regions 3 and 5 take aside, or search/form where that is genuinely what they are, and each carries an accessible name in every state — with two lateral regions, unnamed ones are indistinguishable from each other in a landmark list, which is the specific failure this frame invites.
  • Bypass to the centre. When a lateral region precedes the centre in the document, the frame provides a discoverable way past it — a skip link, or the top region's own first control — so keyboard and screen-reader users are not walked through the criteria on every visit.
  • A control that exposes a region says which region and what state. aria-expanded reflects whether that region is showing, and aria-controls names it only while it is in the document; when it is genuinely gone the attribute is omitted rather than left pointing at nothing. Label by content — "Show capacity and material", not "Toggle right panel".
  • An in-region close is a shortcut, never the canonical control. A control inside a lateral region can hide it but cannot bring it back, so the toggle outside it remains the one that carries aria-expanded and aria-controls, and activating the in-region close moves focus to that toggle. A region dismissed with no focus destination and no visible way back is the collapsed-by-default failure (§12) arriving one interaction later.
  • Heading levels run down the document, not across the tracks. The top region owns the h1; all three body regions start their own sections at h2. Lateral position is not hierarchy, and the centre being dominant does not make its heading a level higher.
  • Each region scrolls to its own focus. Where a region owns its scroll (§13), focus moving into it must bring the focused control into view.
§09

Motion

Conditional — included: the frame's structure changes under §06
  • Collapse and restore are the only motion this frame owns. A lateral region leaves and returns along its own axis — width, not opacity — so the centre visibly takes and gives back the space. A fade reads as content vanishing; a slide reads as a region moving.
  • One region moves at a time. Animating both sides together turns a small change into the whole screen rearranging, and the centre's edges become impossible to track.
  • Use the system's short duration and standard easing. Motion here is orientation, not expression.
  • The Drawer route uses Drawer's own enter and exit, and the frame underneath is not animated as well.
  • Responsive displacement is not animated. A layout re-laying out at a breakpoint is not an event the user caused.
  • The region leaves the document when the motion ends, not when it starts — mounted and aria-hidden on its way out, removed once the width reaches zero, so §06 and the animation do not contradict each other.
  • Reduced motion: the region appears and disappears without transition and leaves the document in the same frame. Nothing in the contract depends on the animation.
§10

Content rules

Conditional — included: the frame only reads correctly if the two sides stay distinct
  • Each lateral region's heading names its own role, in its own words. "Release criteria" and "Capacity and material" are two roles; "Filters" and "More" are one role and an apology. The headings are how a reader learns the frame has two sides on purpose.
  • No control appears on both sides. A duplicated action or field is the first symptom that the two purposes have merged; move it to the side that owns the decision it belongs to.
  • The centre names the subject the sides are about. The three regions share terminology — the same word for a work order, a line, a shift — because the reader is expected to carry a value across two boundaries without translating it.
  • A lateral region reports; it does not become the work. If material in region 3 or 5 grows until it needs its own scroll, its own tabs and its own actions to be usable, it has stopped being lateral and the screen needs splitting.
  • Truncation policy belongs to the sides, wrapping to the centre. Lateral regions are narrow by contract: their content must survive truncation with the meaning intact. The centre is where full values, long descriptions and wide tables are allowed to take the room.
§11

Responsive

Unconditional
  • Wide. All three body regions coexist. Surplus width goes to the centre; bounded lateral tracks do not grow with the viewport.
  • Intermediate. One lateral region leaves the horizontal composition — stacked, conditionally presented, or in a Drawer — and the other stays. Which one leaves is decided by semantic importance to the task, declared once for the screen, and it is the same one every time.
  • Constrained. Both lateral regions leave. The centre keeps priority and the full measure; the displaced regions keep the §08 reading order — the one read before acting above the centre, the one about the centre's state below it.
  • The floor is the trigger. Displacement happens when the centre reaches mainMinWidth, not at a fashionable device width, and never by narrowing all three tracks together.
  • Displacement is not a different layout. Same regions, same contents, same names, same order logic. A constrained-width side that loses half its content was two designs.
  • Collapsible is a user choice, not a breakpoint. Do not hide a side at some width and call it responsive: that is displacement, and it follows the rules above.
Do — the less-used side leaves first

4 · centre, still above its floor
On a tablet at the planning desk the criteria are touched every minute and the capacity read a few times an hour, so region 5 goes to a Drawer and region 3 stays. Priority is measured in use, not in position.

§12

Anti-patterns

Unconditional
  • Three columns because there are three content groups. The frame is justified by two independent lateral roles, not by a count of things to display.
  • The same supplementary role on both sides. Filters left and filters right, context left and context right: one region's worth of material spread over two, and the centre paying for it.
  • Reaching for it when one persistent secondary region is enough. Use sidebar. A second lateral region invented for balance is the most common way this frame is adopted wrongly.
  • Treating global navigation + local navigation + content as this template. Navigation-system architecture is outside this catalog, and a nav shell is not a workspace frame.
  • A lateral collection whose selection replaces the centre. That is master–detail; its contract — selection identity, correspondence, stale content, empty state — is specified nowhere here.
  • Compressing the centre to keep both sides. Named again because it is the failure this layout actually ships with.
  • Lateral regions wider, together, than the centre. The screen then has three peers and no subject.
  • Both sides collapsed by default. A frame whose defining regions start hidden is a single column with two extra controls.
  • Sides reachable only by scrolling past the centre. A region below a long table is not lateral information; it is a footer.
§13

Implementation notes

Unconditional
  • Three tracks, one declaration. Build the body with Grid/GridItem; Container, Surface, Box, Stack and Divider establish the regions and their boundaries. The lateral tracks are fixed or bounded lengths, the centre is minmax(<mainMinWidth>, 1fr). That minmax is the §11 contract expressed as one value — without it the frame silently squeezes instead of displacing.
  • All three tracks need min-width: 0. A wide table or a long part number in any region will otherwise push its track past the declared width and break the composition from inside — with three tracks this is three times as likely and much harder to see.
  • Compose the track list from the regions that are present, rather than declaring three tracks and emptying one. An empty track still takes its width, and the centre never gets it back.
  • Document order is authored, not arranged. Do not place regions with order or explicit grid lines against their DOM sequence; §08's reading order and the visual order must come from the same source.
  • Independent scroll is opt-in and explicit. Either the page scrolls as one, or each region owns its own scroll container with its own height constraint. Three scroll contexts by accident is this frame's signature defect; two of them nested is unusable.
  • A collapsible region's track sizes to its content (auto), so the region's own width transition moves the boundary. Animating grid-template-columns makes the frame's structure the animated thing.
  • A sticky region sticks within its own track, never to the viewport, and never overlaps the centre or its own scroll. The bottom region, if it is anchored, reserves its height rather than covering the last row.
  • The Drawer route is the same content mounted elsewhere, not a second implementation of the region. One component, two homes, or the copies drift within a release.
  • The frame holds only §06's state — per-region collapse and drawer flags. Everything the regions contain holds its own state, and the layout passes nothing between them.
