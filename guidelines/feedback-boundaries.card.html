Toast vs Snackbar vs Alert

Three transient-looking notices, one family, no interchangeability. The deciding factors are not the length of the copy or the severity of the message — they are duration (is this an event or a condition?) and ownership (who holds the notice?). Everything else in the three components follows from those two.

First: event or condition? Something happened once → Toast or Snackbar. Something is true and stays true → Alert, in the page flow, where its position states its scope. Then, for events: is the surface that raised it still on screen and still the thing the user is working in? Yes → Snackbar, mounted in that surface. No → Toast, raised imperatively. Never decide by tone, by length, or by which one “feels lighter”.

Consequences of the boundary

  • A drawer that closes on save cannot confirm the save. The surface unmounts, so the notice belongs to what remains — a Toast.
  • A failure of an action is a Toast; a failure of a system is an Alert. “Export failed” expires; “Historian unreachable” does not.
  • Undo is almost always a Snackbar when the record is still in view: reachability of the action is the whole reason the component exists.
  • “Saved” is never an Alert. A green in-flow block that never leaves reflows the page and outlives the event — the most common misuse in review.
  • Bulk results follow the screen. Still on the list that changed → Snackbar with a count. Navigated away → one Toast with a count.
  • Never two at the same bottom edge. A page-scope Snackbar plus a bottom-right Toaster is the collision to catch; move the toaster to the top.

None of the three

  • A decision the user must take before continuing, or any message with two competing actions. Dialog.
  • A field-level problem. Errors live beside their input, permanently. Field error — an Alert may summarise them, never replace them.
  • A selection’s actions. Several actions, held as long as the selection is — a toolbar, not a bar that expires.
  • An empty region. “No work orders match” is content, not a warning. Empty state.
  • Field help. A hint is not an alert, however small the alert.
  • Something the user can see happen. A row that visibly disappears needs no notice at all.
  • The only record of an operational event. All three are courtesies; the audit trail is the record.
